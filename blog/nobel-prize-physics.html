Every Nobel Prize in Physics, 1901–2026

From Röntgen's X-rays to the IceCube neutrino observatory: the complete, chronological record of who won the Nobel Prize in Physics and what they won it for.

The first Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded in 1901, five years after Alfred Nobel's will set aside his fortune for "those who, during the preceding year, shall have conferred the greatest benefit to humankind." Since then the prize has traced the arc of modern physics: the discovery of radioactivity and the electron, the quantum revolution of the 1920s, the particle zoo of the post-war decades, and, more recently, lasers, topological matter, gravitational waves and the quantum circuits at the heart of today's quantum computers.

Notes

  • Years are the prize year. A few prizes (for example 1917, 1918, 1921 and 1943) were held back and formally presented the following year.
  • No physics prize was awarded in 1916, 1931, 1934 and 1940–1942, mostly because of the World Wars or because no nomination met the criteria.
  • John Bardeen is the only person to have won the physics prize twice (1956 and 1972), so he is counted once in the laureate total.
  • Underlined names are women laureates. Marie Curie also won the 1911 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
  • Prize descriptions are condensed from the official citations. Full citations are at nobelprize.org.
